Comparing TF-IDF and embeddings

Week 9

TF-IDF and sentence embeddings both turn a sentence into a row of numbers. This page compares the two methods on twelve short sentences.

The embeddings come from the model all-MiniLM-L6-v2, which gives each sentence 384 numbers. The TF-IDF rows use TfidfVectorizer(stop_words="english"). All scores were computed in Python and are rounded to 2 decimals.

        Summary
        
  
        
    
          
      
        • A TF-IDF row has one column for each word in the vocabulary, so you can read which words a sentence contains.
          • 
      
        • An embedding has 384 columns with no names. Sentences with related meanings get similar numbers, even when they share no words.
          • 
      
        • cosine_similarity scores both kinds of row in the same way.
          • 
      
        • The stop-word list removes words such as not and nothing. This can change what a TF-IDF row says about a sentence.
          • 
      
        • A high score from either method means similar words or a similar topic. It does not show that two sentences agree.
          • 
      
        • The map is approximate. Use the scores to decide which sentences are nearest.
