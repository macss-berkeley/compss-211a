Stage C · Weighting

TF-IDF matrix

A term scores high when it is frequent in this document but rare across documents.

TF = count of the term in the document DF = number of documents containing the term IDF = ln(N ÷ DF), which grows as DF shrinks N = 4 documents weight = TF × IDF

A term in all 4 documents gets IDF = ln(4/4) = 0: weight zero, no matter how frequent. Darker gold = higher weight; the outlined cell is the strongest term shown for that document.

TF-IDF weight matrix

scikit-learn’s default TfidfVectorizer differs in details (smoothing, normalization); the ranking logic is the same.

Before Friday’s quiz

Six lines to keep

  • Tokenization decides what counts as a word: The ≠ the until you lowercase, and week. ≠ week until you strip punctuation.
  • Stopword lists are a choice. A default list can delete meaning: not working becomes working.
  • Bag-of-words discards order. Bigrams (n-grams) recover short phrases like not working, but only words that survive filtering can form them.
  • TF-IDF = frequent here, rare elsewhere. A term in every document scores zero; a term shared by two documents can’t tell them apart.
  • Top terms describe the corpus you built, not the world. Four synthetic comments are not evidence about Berkeley students or campus opinion.
  • Interpretation limits: a model cannot use evidence that preprocessing removed, so write claims that survive your preprocessing choices.
