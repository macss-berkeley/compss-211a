How Computers Learn Word Meanings

Week 9 · Embeddings & Clustering ≈ 8 minutes Hand-made miniature

An embedding turns meaning into position: words that appear in similar contexts end up pointing in similar directions. Everything below is a hand-placed two-dimensional miniature. Real embeddings have hundreds of dimensions and are learned from co-occurrence, but the geometry works the same way.

4·Whose contexts?

On this map, “morning” sits between the food and study words because we drew it there. Real embeddings get their positions from the texts they are trained on, and they pick up social associations the same way. That is how this week’s reading (Caliskan et al., Science 2017) measured stereotypes inside word vectors. Treat similarity as evidence about the training text, not as a fact about people, and say so when you interpret results.

✓Before Friday’s quiz

  • A word’s position is learned from its contexts: similar contexts, similar directions.
  • Cosine similarity compares directions (1 same, 0 unrelated, −1 opposite); vector length doesn’t matter.
  • A document can be summarized as a position too. The lab’s route is TF-IDF, then TruncatedSVD (LSA).
  • KMeans always returns k clusters. A fixed random_state makes runs repeatable, nothing more.
  • Read cluster members before naming clusters. A label is a claim you check against the documents.
  • Similarity reflects the training text. State that limitation when you make social claims.
