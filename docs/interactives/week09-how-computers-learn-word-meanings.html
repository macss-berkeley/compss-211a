How computers learn word meanings

Week 9 · Embeddings & clustering ≈ 8 minutes Small hand-made example

An embedding gives each word a position, stored as a list of numbers called a vector. Words that appear in similar contexts (the words around them) end up pointing in similar directions. The map below is a small example we placed by hand. It has two dimensions, so each word has two numbers. Real embeddings have hundreds of dimensions and are learned from which words appear near each other in lots of text. The math works the same way.

2·A document as an average of its words

If you average the vectors of all the words in a document, the document gets a position too. It shows up on the map above as a star. Friday’s lab uses the same idea: you start with TF-IDF vectors and shrink them to fewer dimensions with TruncatedSVD (also called LSA). Each document ends up with a position.

4·Embeddings reflect their training text

On this map, “morning” sits between the food and study words because we put it there. Real embeddings get their positions from the text they are trained on. So they also pick up the social associations in that text, including stereotypes. This week’s reading (Caliskan et al., Science 2017) used this to measure stereotypes in word vectors. When two words are similar, that tells you something about the training text. It is not a fact about people. Say this when you interpret your results.

✓Summary

  • A word’s position is learned from its contexts, the words around it. Words with similar contexts point in similar directions.
  • Cosine similarity compares directions. 1 means the same direction, 0 means unrelated, and −1 means opposite. The length of the vector doesn’t matter.
  • A document can have a position too. In the lab you get it with TF-IDF, then TruncatedSVD (LSA).
  • KMeans always returns k clusters. Setting random_state to a fixed number makes every run give the same result. It does not make the clusters more correct.
  • Read the members of a cluster before you name it. A name is a claim, so check it against the documents.
  • Similarity reflects the training text. Say so when you use it to make claims about society.
